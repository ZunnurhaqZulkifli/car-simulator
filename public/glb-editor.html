      User guide

      What it does

      drive.html can only drive a model once it knows which part is which: what to paint, which wheels spin, where the steering wheel and the lamps are. The upload template says it in each part's own name (paint_1, wheel_tyre_3), so any model named that way can drive without patterns written for it. This editor gives the parts those names, checks the model the way drive.html's loader will read it, and exports the renamed .glb.

      Nothing is saved or uploaded. Your work only lives in this tab: export before you close or reload it.

      Step by step

      1. Open a model. Pick a built-in car, press Open .glb…, or drop a .glb anywhere on the page.
      2. Set its real length, bumper to bumper in metres. drive.html scales every model to its length, so the checks measure it at that size. Each grid square is 50 cm.
      3. Point the nose along the blue arrow. If it faces another way, press Turn +90°, 180° or −90° until it doesn't.
      4. Pick parts. Click one on the car or in the Parts list. Shift-click (or ⌘/Ctrl-click) adds another, or takes a picked one back out. Esc lets go of them all. Picked parts show blue, through everything in front of them.
      5. Tag them. Press a role button under Parts: each picked part is renamed to that role's next free name (Paint gives paint_1, then paint_2…) and coloured as it.
      6. Work down the Template check until nothing is ✕ and it says Fits the template.
      7. Press Export .glb. It downloads <name>-template.glb: every part with your names, hidden ones too, in the model's own materials.

      A built-in car opens checked by Its drive.html patterns, which is how drive.html reads it today, and tagging is off there. Press Rename all to template to name every part from those patterns, then tag as above.

      Reading the Template check

      ✓ fine · ! it drives, but goes without something (indicators, tinted glass) · ✕ drive.html gets it wrong, or can't load the model · i for your information.

      • Size: at its real length the model should be 1.4–2.3 m wide and 0.9–2.2 m tall. Bigger means something outside the car (a floor, a stand, a stray part) stretches it, or the nose isn't along the arrow.
      • Facing: the steering wheel (or else the headlights) must be in the front half.
      • Drive side: whichever side the steering wheel is on. drive.html keeps the cabin as modelled, so a left-hand-drive car is driven from the left.
      • Tyres: one in each corner. Each corner's tyres say where its hub is (the green boxes).
      • Wheels: everything tagged Wheel spins, so it must stay inside the wheels. A part reaching outside them would spin too: split it and tag only the wheel piece.
      • Steering wheel: tag only the wheel (about 0.30–0.40 m across). The orange ring is the rim drive.html found and turns the wheel about; the blue dot is where the driver's eye goes.
      • Headlights / Tail lights: lamp parts on both sides in the front (or rear) 60 cm get the indicators, beam and brake glows (the yellow boxes).
      • Paint / Glass: without them the body keeps the model's own colour and the windows aren't tinted.

      Tools

      • Hide / show (H) hides the picked parts so you can see past them; the ◉ in the list hides one. Hidden parts still export. Show all brings them all back.
      • Split loose pieces makes each separate piece of the picked parts a part of its own, named after it with _1, _2… (biggest first), e.g. to get a steering wheel out of the dashboard it was modelled into. A part in one piece, or in more than 400, stays whole: cut it apart in Blender.
      • Delete takes the picked parts out of the model and the export.
      • Rename: type in a part's name in the list and press Enter. A name starting with a role's prefix gives it that role.
      • Colour by role paints each part in its role's colour; off shows the model's own materials. It only changes the view: the export always has the model's own materials.
      • An opened .glb's groups named like a role (paint_…) are renamed group_1, group_2…, so only part names carry roles.

      Undo and redo

      Undo⌘Z (Ctrl+Z on Windows), or the Undo button
      Redo⇧⌘Z (Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y), or the Redo button
      • Each change to the model is one step: tagging, renaming, Rename all to template, hiding and showing parts, Delete, Split loose pieces, a turn, and the real length.
      • Undo puts the model back exactly as it was before the step: names, hidden, deleted and split parts, which way it faces, its length and the patterns it's checked by. The parts picked then are picked again, the checks run again, and the status line says what was undone. Hover the buttons to see what they'll undo or redo.
      • Redo does an undone step again. Changing anything after an undo drops the steps you undid: they can't be redone.
      • The last 100 steps are kept. Opening another model starts afresh.
      • Not steps: picking, Colour by role, switching patterns by hand, moving the view and exporting.
      • While you're typing in a box (a part's name, the real length), ⌘Z undoes your typing there, as anywhere. Click off the box to undo a step.

      Moving around

      Drag to turn the view, Shift-drag or right-drag to move it, scroll or pinch to zoom. A click that doesn't drag picks the part under it, skipping hidden ones.

      After exporting

      drive.html doesn't read template names by itself yet: to drive the model, put the .glb in public/models/ and give it a CARS entry in drive.html that uses the template's patterns.
